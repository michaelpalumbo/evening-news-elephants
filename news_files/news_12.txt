Pedologists around the globe are sounding the alarms after soil samples in remote regions of Europe, Australia, North America, South America are testing positive for abnormally high amounts of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, or PAHs. These contaminants, usually caused by contact with petroleum products, have not yet been recorded outside of urban soil samples. Some experts hypothesize that the contamination occurred due to the global circulation of sand and other aerosols originating from Iran, following the attack on the country's oil infrastructure. While further testing is needed, this contamination could have major consequences for both native ecosystems and agricultural systems. There is some concern that the levels in these regions could cause acid rain levels that have not been witnessed since the early 2000s.
