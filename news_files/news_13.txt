Bjork’s new album, titled Himinn-Niour, will be released next Friday. The artist states the
album is inspired by the “deliciously hazardous and brutal” nature of atmospheric
instability, and will include collaborations with artists such as Moses Sumney and bbno$
(pronounced baby no money)

The Government of Canada has officially released a statement announcing that the
Canadian Pension Plan no longer has any stakes invested in the production or
distribution of any AI companies or manufacturing products, including Novorim. The
announcement comes after their phones had reportedly been flooded with calls for
months from concerned citizens fearing their pensions might get caught up in the
predicted AI bubble burst. No other portfolio changes were announced during the
statement.

Award winning, New York based immersive theatre company PunchDrunk premieres
their newest work, “Drive your Plow over the bones of the dead” next month. The play,
an adaptation of Nobel Prize-winning author Olga Tokarczuk’s book of the same name,
takes place on a 12 acres estate 80 minutes North of New York City that has been
transformed into a replica of the Polish countryside. Tickets are already sold out for the
current run, but started at $432+taxes.

The class action lawsuit against AI security company Flock begins tomorrow. If the
claimants win, The lawsuit is set to be one of the largest class action suits in American
history, surpassing the payouts of both BP’s payout after the Gulf of Mexico spill, and
Big Tabacco’s loss in 1998.

The Australian Government has announced they will officially be returning governmental
authority of the island state of Tasmania back to its original Indigenous caretakers, a
people that today identify as the Palawa or Panaka. This is the latest in a string of
court-mandated ceedings of territorial control in the country, and is the largest land
transfer in the country to date.
