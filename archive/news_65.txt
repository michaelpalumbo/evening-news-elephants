Debates continue within the UN Security Council as to whether a country representative for the Latin American and Caribbean States and African States groups will be granted permanent member status within the council. The deliberation was sparked by UN Secretary General Terence Munoz, after he threatened resignation if the current permanent nations did not consider the council expansion. Currently, the permanent council member nations include the United States, China, Russia, France and the United Kingdom. These states have veto power over any and all motions that move through the council, creating a significant global power. Critics argue that an overrepresentation of Western powers maintains imperial legacies. In a statement to the press, Munoz confirmed he was "ready to see the UN move past these historical inequities." Voting on the motion is set to occur later next week.
