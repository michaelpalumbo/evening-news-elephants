At least five journalists have disappeared in Burkina Faso (West Africa near Senegal) during the past week, part of a concerning trend in the crackdown on civil liberties in the country. Since a military coup seized power in 2022, Burkina Faso has been under the leadership of Captain Ibrahim Traoré. In April 2026, Traoré told the press that "Everyone is free to say what they want and give their own opinion." Despite this, four journalists had already been abducted and forced into military conscription, only released in July of that year after the Federation of African Journalists (FAJ) and the International Federation of Journalists (IFJ) campaigned for their release. The journalist groups, along with Amnesty International and Human Rights Watch, have renewed calls on the junta for transparency regarding the location of the missing journalists, as it is suspected they are being wrongfully detained, and possibly tortured.
