A government investigation has found that MailConnext, a private company that offers AI mail observation to prisons, has been using prisoner correspondence to train their AI models, without correspondent consent. Canada has had a service agreement with the company since June, and has reported a 12% decrease in mail sorting times since partnering with MailConnext. As opposed to reading through all mail that enters the prison, correctional officers currently take photos of prisoners' mail, and scan it through the MailConnext app, to ensure it did not violate the prison's mail policies. Correctional Services Canada said in a press statement that while prisoners and their loved ones must consent to having their mail screened by MailConnext, the consent form originally stated that the data from the mail was not stored within the software. Correctional Services Canada has not yet confirmed whether they will end their partnership with MailConnext.
