Increasing accounts of botulism related to at-home canning is being reported across Canada and the United States. Largely attributed to the fresh produce shortage caused by fertilizer supply chain issues worldwide, a large number of the population has taken to attempts at in-home food preservation, with "gardening 101" and "easy canning recipes" Google searches going up by 265% and 143% respectively within the last 6 months. The CDC is currently investigating whether or not faulty information might be a possible cause of increased cases of illness, as a rise in AI cookbooks and how-to guides have flooded independent publishing sites such as Amazon. Although no official connection has been established, the CDC reminds consumers to only can in sterile, canning-safe jars with new seals, and to only can foods on their, "Safe Canning List." They caution against listening to self-proclaimed "Rebel Canners" on social media, reminding people to never can meat or dairy.
